1. What is Omenala? The Igbo Philosophy of Custom and Law

Category: Culture | 6 min read | Nov 2026

Omenala means the custom of the land. It is the unwritten constitution of Igbo people. It tells us how to greet elders, how to settle land disputes, how to marry, how to bury, how to do business with honesty. Omenala is not just tradition — it is law. When an Igbo elder says "Omenala adighi ekwe" — it means the custom does not allow it. In our project, Omenala Web3, we use this same principle: do what is right, even when no one is watching. That is why we lock tokens, show transparency, and teach before we sell. Omenala teaches that a person without character has nothing, even if he has money.

Takeaway: Omenala = Do right. Ofo na Ogu = Truth and Innocence.

2. Eke, Orie, Afo, Nkwo: The Four Igbo Market Days Explained

Category: Market Days | 5 min read

Before Monday to Sunday, Igbo people used four market days. Eke is Fire — day of beginning, kings, new markets. Orie is Water — day of flow and trade. Afo is Earth — day of farm and grounding. Nkwo is Air — day of spirit and movement. Our base date is Oct 27, 2026 = Eke. The cycle repeats every 4 days. So Nov 8, 2026 is Eke again. Each community has its own market day. If you know your birthday, you can know your market day. This system helped our fathers plan farming, trading, festivals and meetings without calendar.

3. Ofo na Ogu: The Igbo System of Justice and Truth

Category: Justice | 6 min read

Ofo is a small staff, symbol of truth and authority. Ogu is symbol of innocence. When two people quarrel, elders bring Ofo. Whoever holds Ofo must speak truth — if you lie holding Ofo, Ala (Earth) will punish you. This was our court before courts existed. Ofo na Ogu still matters today in Web3: we must be transparent. That is why Omenala shows its contract, its locks, its team. No hiding.

5. New Yam Festival (Iri Ji): History, Ritual and Meaning

Category: Festival | 5 min read

Iri Ji is thanksgiving. Before eating new yam, the community thanks Ala for harvest. The eldest man cuts the first yam. No one eats new yam before the festival. That is Omenala. It teaches gratitude, sharing, and respect for farmers. In many villages, Iri Ji is in August or September.
